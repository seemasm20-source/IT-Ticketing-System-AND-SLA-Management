# 📜 What is a Service Level Agreement (SLA)?

[ Back to Overview] → [README.md](./README.md)

---

## 📋 Summary

An **SLA (Service Level Agreement)** is a formal agreement between a service provider and its customer. It defines the **service standards** the customer can expect, such as **response times**, **resolution times** and **availability**, usually for each **priority level** of ticket. The provider can be an internal IT help desk or an outside company, and the customer is the business or its users.

---

## 💡 Why SLAs Matter

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Sets expectations** | Users know how long they will wait for a response and a fix |
| **Measures performance** | The IT team is held to agreed timeframes, and the results can be reported |
| **Prioritises work** | Critical tickets are handled before low-priority ones |
| **Protects the business** | Critical systems are restored quickly, which limits lost work and money |
| **Guides escalation** | When a ticket is close to its target time, it is escalated to the next level |

---

## 🔑 Key SLA Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Response time** | How quickly the help desk first acknowledges and starts working on a ticket |
| **Resolution time** | How quickly the problem is fixed and the ticket is resolved |
| **Priority** | How urgent the ticket is, based on its impact and urgency |
| **Impact** | How many users or how much of the business the problem affects |
| **Urgency** | How quickly the problem must be fixed |
| **Business hours** | The times the SLA clock runs, for example Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 17:00 |
| **SLA clock** | The timer that counts the response and resolution time. It can pause while the help desk waits for the user |
| **Breach** | A ticket that missed its target time |
| **Escalation** | Passing a ticket to a higher support level or a manager, before or after a breach |
| **Availability** | The percentage of time a service is working, for example 99.9% |

---

## 🎫 Example: Priority Levels and Targets

Every organisation sets its own priorities and times. This table is an **example**.

| Priority | Example situation | Response time | Resolution time |
|----------|-------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **P1 - Critical** | A system is down and many users cannot work | 15 minutes | 4 hours |
| **P2 - High** | A major function is not working, or one important user is blocked | 1 hour | 8 hours |
| **P3 - Medium** | A problem with a workaround, or one user is affected | 4 hours | 3 business days |
| **P4 - Low** | A request or a small problem | 1 business day | 5 business days |

> **Priority = impact + urgency.** A problem that affects many users and must be fixed now is critical. A small problem that affects one user and can wait is low.

---

## 🧩 SLA, OLA and UC

| Agreement | Between | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **SLA** (Service Level Agreement) | The IT provider and the customer | The help desk answers a P2 ticket within 1 hour |
| **OLA** (Operational Level Agreement) | Teams inside the same company | The network team fixes a firewall request for the help desk within 4 hours |
| **UC** (Underpinning Contract) | The IT provider and an outside supplier | The internet provider restores a failed line within 8 hours |

---

## 🛠️ Where Tier 1 and Tier 2 Work with SLAs

| Role | SLA responsibilities |
|------|---------------------|
| **Tier 1** | Logs the ticket with the right priority, gives a first response within the response time, fixes common problems, and escalates when a ticket cannot be fixed in time |
| **Tier 2** | Takes escalated tickets, investigates deeper, and works to the resolution time |
| **Both** | Update the ticket regularly, record what was done, and tell the user about any delay |
